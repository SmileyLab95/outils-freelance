What this agreement covers

  • The work and the main contract: what you hand over, the deliverables and dates, and the requirements of your client contract that you pass on in writing. You stay responsible to your client.
  • Payment: a fixed fee or a day or hourly rate, paid within the agreed days of the invoice, whether or not your client has paid you.
  • Ownership: the copyright is assigned to you in a signed writing once you have paid, so that you can transfer it to your client.
  • Tax forms: a Form W-9 before the first payment, and a Form 1099-NEC when the law requires it.
  • Confidentiality and your client: the subcontractor keeps your client’s information confidential and, if you tick the option, does not approach your client for 12 months.

Freelance Isn’t Free: you are the hiring party

When you hire another freelancer in New York State or New York City for $800 or more (alone or with contracts with the same freelancer over the previous 120 days), the law requires you to give them a written contract and keep a copy. It must include both parties’ names and mailing addresses, an itemized list of the services, their value, the rate and method of pay, the payment date, and the date by which the freelancer must submit their invoice so you can meet your payment deadline. Fill in those fields and the generated agreement contains them. Illinois has a similar rule from $500 over 120 days. See the Freelance Isn’t Free guide, and prepare your own contract with your client with the freelance contract.

What this template does not cover

Construction work, government contracts, staffing agencies, and subcontractors based outside the United States. Whether a non-solicitation clause can be enforced depends on the state: have it reviewed by an attorney before you rely on it.
