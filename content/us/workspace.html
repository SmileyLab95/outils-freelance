Everything a one-person business needs

  • Estimates and invoices laid out like the invoice generator: sales tax line, PO number, numbers in sequence (2026-001, 2026-002…), PDF and a prefilled email.
  • Payments: partial payments, overdue invoices flagged, a reminder email ready to send; credit memos to cancel or correct an invoice you sent.
  • Income recorded on the cash method: each payment on the date you receive it, totals by quarter and for the year, refunds on line 2, export to CSV.
  • Expenses sorted by Schedule C line, with the IRS description of each line and meals at 50%; export to CSV.
  • Schedule C summary for the year, line by line, with a CSV export: a preparation aid, not a tax return.
  • Estimated taxes: your next quarterly payment, worked out like the quarterly tax calculator, the payments you made, and the Forms 1099-NEC you should receive.
  • Time tracking: a timer or quick entries in hours or days, billable time by client, and an invoice drafted from your time in one click.
  • Leads: your pipeline by stage, today's follow-ups, a do-not-contact list, and the federal rules for prospecting by email and phone.
  • Cash flow: what is already owed in federal tax, your projected balance over 3, 6 or 12 months, late clients and your average time to get paid.
  • Deadlines: the federal tax calendar for your situation, with the estimated amount of your next quarterly payment.

Not in this version: accrual accounting, inventory and cost of goods sold, depreciation, vehicle details (Part IV of Schedule C), state sales tax and state income tax, LLCs taxed as corporations, and employees.

For a one-off invoice without saving anything, the invoice generator is still there. To see every deadline and add it to your calendar, open the tax calendar.

Tax amounts are estimates; you file and pay with the IRS. For information only, not tax advice.
