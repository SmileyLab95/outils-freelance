      How self-employment tax is calculated

      As a freelancer, independent contractor or sole proprietor, you pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare yourself. The IRS applies the rate to 92.35% of your net profit, which mirrors the employer half being deductible for a business.

      SE tax = net profit × 92.35% × 15.3%　(12.4% Social Security up to $184,500 + 2.9% Medicare)

      Then federal income tax is calculated on your adjusted gross income, after the deduction for half of your self-employment tax, the standard deduction and the qualified business income (QBI) deduction.

      What this calculator does not include

      • Tax credits (child tax credit, education credits…) and itemized deductions.
      • Retirement contributions (SEP-IRA, Solo 401(k)) and the self-employed health insurance deduction, which can lower your income tax.
      • Exact state and local income taxes: the state line is a flat-rate estimate.
