      Before you buy: three checks

      1. Trademarks. An available domain can still use someone else's trademark. Search the USPTO trademark database for registered and pending federal trademarks; a full clearance search also covers state trademark registers and unregistered use online. Registering a business name with your state does not give you federal trademark rights.
      2. The rules of the extension. .dev and .app sites only open over HTTPS.
      3. The same name everywhere. If you can, use the same name for your business, website, email address and social accounts: clients find you more easily.

      Where to register your name

      Registries do not sell names themselves: you buy them from an accredited registrar, or a reseller. The official list is ICANN's list of accredited registrars, for .com, .net, .org, .pro, .dev and the other generic extensions.

      Compare the first-year price with the renewal price, and what is included (email address, privacy of your contact details). We earn no commission on these links.

      Choosing well

      • Short and easy to say: a name people get right over the phone, with no tricky spelling.
      • No hyphen if you can avoid it, unless it makes the name clearer (“jane-smith”).
      • In your own name: register yourself as the registrant, even if someone else builds your website.
      • Automatic renewal: a name that is not renewed can be lost and bought by someone else.
      • A professional email address: with your domain, create an address like hello@yourname.com, with your registrar or an email service.
