    State programs you can opt into

    • California, Disability Insurance Elective Coverage (DIEC): for sole proprietors, independent contractors, partners and the managing members of certain LLCs who don't pay into State Disability Insurance. It covers Disability Insurance, up to 39 weeks when you can't work because of a non-work-related illness, injury or pregnancy, and Paid Family Leave, up to 8 weeks in a 12-month period. For 2026, the premium is 8.84% of the net profit on your 2024 tax return (Schedule SE line 3 or Schedule C line 31), with a minimum of $406.64 a year for a net profit of $4,600 or less, paid in four quarterly installments. You must be in the plan for 6 months before you can apply for benefits. Details and the application: EDD, Disability Insurance Elective Coverage.
    • Minnesota, Paid Leave: self-employed people and independent contractors can apply for coverage for at least 104 consecutive weeks. Coverage starts in the calendar quarter after approval, and you can only leave on January 1, with a notice filed at least 30 days before. The yearly premium is the Paid Leave premium rate times your self-employment premium base, capped at the Social Security wage base; benefits work as for employees, figured on that premium base instead of wages. See Minnesota Paid Leave, opt in for the current rate.
    • New York, Paid Family Leave and disability benefits: without employees, you opt in by buying an insurance policy, and it must cover both Paid Family Leave and disability: you can't buy Paid Family Leave alone. The insurer sets the price. See New York Paid Family Leave for self-employed individuals.
    • Oregon, Paid Leave Oregon: if your work is in Oregon and you earned at least $1,000 of Oregon net income from self-employment in the previous tax year. The 2025-2026 rate is 0.6% of that income, on up to $184,500, paid every quarter, and you agree to pay for at least 3 years. You then get the same benefits as employees. See Paid Leave Oregon for the self-employed.
    • Washington, Paid Family and Medical Leave: you elect coverage for at least 3 years, then for periods of at least 1 year, for both family and medical leave, and you pay the full employee premium. You can take benefits after working 820 hours in the state after filing your notice. You can withdraw within 30 days after the end of a coverage period. See Washington Paid Leave, elective coverage.

    Other states have their own programs, or none: check your state's labor or employment department before you rely on any coverage.

    Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)

    SSDI is a federal program for long-term disability, funded by Social Security taxes, including the self-employment tax you pay on your net earnings. Benefits start after a waiting period of 5 full months of disability. You need enough recent covered work: generally 20 quarters of coverage in the 40 quarters before you became disabled, fewer if you're under 31. It doesn't cover a short illness or time off to care for a child.

    Before you opt in

    • These programs are a commitment: you pay every quarter, often for several years, even if you never claim.
    • Private disability insurance can cover the rest of your income: see the business insurance guide, and build a cash buffer with the set-aside calculator.

    Guidance only, based on the official pages and laws of each state and the Social Security Act read in November 2026. Premiums, waiting periods and benefit amounts change: the state agency decides on your coverage and your claim. Massachusetts, Colorado and other states with their own programs aren't covered yet.
