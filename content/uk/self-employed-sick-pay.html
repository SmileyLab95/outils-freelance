    If you're too ill to work

    • No Statutory Sick Pay for self-employed work: SSP (up to £123.25 a week for up to 28 weeks) is paid by an employer. If you also have a job, ask your employer.
    • New Style Employment and Support Allowance (ESA): if you're under State Pension age, have a health condition or disability that affects how much you can work, and have paid enough National Insurance, usually in the last 2 to 3 years (credits count too). It's open to the self-employed.
    • How much: the "assessment rate" for the first 13 weeks while your claim is assessed, up to £75.65 a week if you're under 25 and up to £95.55 if you're 25 or over. After the assessment: up to £95.55 a week in the work-related activity group, or up to £145.90 in the support group. A private pension over £85 a week reduces ESA by half of the amount above £85.

    If you're having a baby

    • Maternity Allowance, not Statutory Maternity Pay: SMP comes from an employer. If you're self-employed, you can get Maternity Allowance (MA) if you've been registered as self-employed (or employed) for at least 26 weeks in the 66 weeks before your baby is due.
    • How much: between £27 and £194.32 a week for up to 39 weeks. You get the maximum if you've also paid Class 2 National Insurance for at least 13 of the 66 weeks: £7,578.48 over 39 weeks. With no Class 2 paid, it's £27 a week, or £1,053 over 39 weeks. In between, the amount depends on the number of weeks paid.
    • Topping up: after you apply, HMRC tells you how many more weeks of Class 2 you need to pay. Voluntary Class 2 is £3.65 a week in 2026/27, so 13 weeks cost £47.45 at most (weeks in an earlier tax year are charged at that year's rate). Your MA is then increased and backdated.
    • When to claim: once you've been pregnant for 26 weeks, with form MA1 and proof of your due date (MATB1 certificate or a letter from your doctor or midwife). Payments can start from the 11th week before your baby is due. Claim within 3 months of your chosen start date to get the full amount. You should get a decision within 20 working days.
    • No tax: you don't pay income tax or National Insurance on Maternity Allowance. It reduces Universal Credit by the same amount.

    If your work stops

    • No New Style Jobseeker's Allowance on Class 2 alone: JSA needs Class 1 contributions as an employee, usually for the previous 2 tax years. Self-employed people who only paid Class 2 aren't eligible, unless they worked as a share fisherman or a volunteer development worker.
    • Universal Credit: you may be able to get it instead, or alongside New Style JSA or ESA (which then reduce it). It isn't covered by this tool: check if you're eligible on GOV.UK.

    Class 2 National Insurance since April 2024

    You no longer have to pay Class 2. If your profits are at least the Small Profits Threshold (£7,105 in 2026/27, £6,845 in 2025/26), HMRC gives you a National Insurance credit instead, and you keep access to contributory benefits, including the State Pension. Below the threshold, you can pay Class 2 voluntarily (£3.65 a week). The Maternity Allowance guide on GOV.UK talks about weeks of Class 2 "paid" and doesn't say how these credits count: this tool doesn't assume they do. HMRC contacts you after you apply if you've paid less than 13 weeks.

    Before you claim

    • Check your National Insurance record for gaps: Check your National Insurance record on GOV.UK.
    • Read the official guides: ESA, Maternity Allowance (with the test period calculator) and Jobseeker's Allowance.
    • In Northern Ireland, these benefits have their own rules: check nidirect.

    State benefits rarely replace your income. Insurance can cover the gap: see the business insurance guide, and build a cash buffer with the set-aside calculator.
