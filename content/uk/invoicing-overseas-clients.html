    What to do

      The rule in one sentence

      For a service, VAT generally follows the client's country when the client is a business, and your country when the client is a private person, with exceptions for consultancy-type services to private clients outside the UK, digital services and services tied to a place (property, events, transport).

      Good to know

      • Check that your client is a business before leaving VAT off: a VAT number, a company registration or their website. Without evidence, the private-client rules apply.
      • Digital services to EU consumers have no threshold for UK businesses: EU VAT applies from the first sale.
      • Flat Rate Scheme: how these sales count in your flat rate turnover isn't covered here; check VAT Notice 733.
      • Payment terms and currency are yours to agree: put them in your contract, together with who pays the bank fees.

      US clients: Form W-8BEN

      A client in the United States will often ask you for a Form W-8BEN. You give it to your client, not to the IRS: it shows you're not a US person. Fill it in before the first payment.

      • Work done from the UK: pay to a non-US resident for work done outside the United States isn't US-source income, so there's no US withholding on it.
      • Without the form, your client may have to withhold 30% of your payments.
      • Who fills it in: an individual, sole traders included; a company uses Form W-8BEN-E.
      • How long it lasts: until 31 December of the third year after you sign it (a form signed in 2026 lasts until the end of 2029), unless your circumstances change, which calls for a new form.
      • Work done in the United States: that part is US-source income, and an exemption is claimed on a different form (Form 8233).

      Information for guidance only, based on HMRC's VAT Notices 741A, 700/1 and 700/12 and its place of supply manual, and for Form W-8BEN on the IRS instructions and Publication 515. For an unusual situation, check with HMRC or an accountant.
