What this agreement covers

  • The work and the main contract: what you hand over, the deliverables and dates, and the requirements of your client contract that you pass on in writing (deadlines, quality, security, confidentiality). You stay responsible to your client.
  • Payment: a fixed fee or a day or hourly rate, paid within the agreed days of the invoice, whether or not your client has paid you.
  • Ownership: the copyright is assigned to you once you have paid, so that you can assign it to your client.
  • Confidentiality and your client: the subcontractor keeps your client’s information confidential and, if you tick the option, does not approach your client for 12 months.
  • Ending early: if your client ends the main contract, you can end this one and pay for the work done.

Before you subcontract

  • Check that your client contract allows you to subcontract, and whether your client must approve the subcontractor.
  • If your subcontractor works through their own limited company, read the off-payroll working (IR35) guidance: the rules depend on the size of the end client.
  • If the subcontractor will handle personal data for your client, put data processing terms in writing first, as the UK GDPR requires.
  • Prepare your own contract with your client with the freelance contract.

What this template does not cover

Construction work (Construction Industry Scheme), public sector contracts, recruitment agencies and staffing, and subcontractors based outside the UK. Have it reviewed by a solicitor for a large or risky project.
