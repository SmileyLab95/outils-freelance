      Before you buy: three checks

      1. Trade marks and company names. An available domain can still use someone else's trade mark. Search the UK trade mark register and the Companies House register: a company name cannot be the same as an existing one, and registering a company name does not protect it as a trade mark.
      2. The rules of the extension. Anyone may register a .uk or .co.uk name; if your address is outside the UK, you must also give a UK address for service (not a PO box). .dev and .app sites only open over HTTPS.
      3. The same name everywhere. If you can, use the same name for your business, website, email address and social accounts: clients find you more easily.

      Where to register your name

      Registries do not sell names themselves: you buy them from an accredited registrar, or a reseller. The official lists:

      • The UK Domain, Nominet's site, which compares registrars for .uk and .co.uk names;
      • ICANN's list of accredited registrars, for .com, .net, .org and the other generic extensions.

      Compare the first-year price with the renewal price, and what is included (email address, privacy of your contact details). We earn no commission on these links.

      Choosing well

      • Short and easy to say: a name people get right over the phone, with no tricky spelling.
      • No hyphen if you can avoid it, unless it makes the name clearer (“jane-smith”).
      • In your own name: register yourself as the registrant, even if someone else builds your website.
      • Automatic renewal: a name that is not renewed can be lost and bought by someone else.
      • A professional email address: with your domain, create an address like hello@yourname.co.uk, with your registrar or an email service.
