The rule: sterling figures for VAT

You can invoice in any currency, but sales must be converted into sterling for VAT, when you record them. If UK VAT is due, your invoice must also show in sterling the total net value at each VAT rate and the VAT at each rate (not each line). There are two standard ways to convert: the UK market selling rate at the time of supply (newspaper rates are acceptable), or the HMRC period rate of exchange, the same rate for a whole period, usually a calendar month. Using another rate needs HMRC's approval.

The rest of the invoice still has to meet the usual rules: see what to put on an invoice and, for a client abroad, invoicing overseas clients (reverse charge, outside the scope of UK VAT).

Where the rate comes from

When you click “Get the HMRC rate”, your browser downloads the month's rates file straight from HMRC's exchange rates service, without going through our servers; the amount is never sent (privacy). If the service doesn't answer, enter the rate from that page, or the UK market selling rate.
