How the comparison works

On the employee side, the calculator starts from your salary and takes off Class 1 National Insurance and Income Tax (Scottish rates if you live in Scotland), with the personal allowance tapered above £100,000. Your employer's National Insurance and pension contributions are paid on top of your salary: they aren't part of your income, unless you add the pension to compare like with like.

On the freelance side, it looks for the turnover that leaves the same income as a sole trader, after your expenses, Class 4 National Insurance and Income Tax on your profit. Divided by your billed days, it gives the equivalent day rate. The self-employed tax calculator shows the full detail, and the sole trader vs limited company comparison what changes with a company.
