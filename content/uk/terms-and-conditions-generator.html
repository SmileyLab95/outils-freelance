What these terms cover

  • For all your customers: who you are and how to contact you, how the contract is formed from your quote, prices and payment, how you carry out the services, intellectual property, liability, ending the contract, complaints and the governing law.
  • With business customers: statutory interest and fixed compensation for late payment under the Late Payment Act, a period to check deliverables, and a limit of liability, which must be reasonable.
  • With consumers: prices including any VAT, the 14-day right to cancel a contract made at a distance or away from your premises, with the official model cancellation form in an annex, and a reminder of their rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. No limit of liability, deemed acceptance or “entire agreement” term applies to them.

How to use them

  • Send your terms with every quote, as a PDF, before the customer accepts it, and ask them to accept the quote in writing: this is what makes the terms part of the contract. Prepare your quotes with the quote and invoice generator.
  • Describe in each quote the services, the price, the timescale and, if you keep the intellectual property, how the customer may use the deliverables.
  • Keep each dated version: the terms in force on the day a quote is accepted apply to that contract.
  • For a large project, add a freelance contract signed by both parties.

With consumers, also

  • Before they are bound, give them the key information: who you are, your address and contact details, the services, the total price including any VAT, payment and timing, how you handle complaints and, where it applies, their right to cancel. Your quote and these terms together cover it.
  • Confirm the contract on a durable medium, such as email, before you start; for a contract agreed at their home, give the information and the cancellation form on paper (or by email if they agree).
  • Do not start during the 14 days unless they expressly ask you to, and ask them to acknowledge that they will lose the right to cancel once the work is complete. If they cancel after asking you to start, they pay for the work done in proportion to the whole contract.
  • If they order on your website, the order button must say “order with obligation to pay” or something equally clear, and your site must show the accepted payment methods from the start of the ordering process.
  • Complaints: reply as quickly as possible. There is no general duty to join an alternative dispute resolution (ADR) scheme; if you belong to one, tell consumers about it when you reply to their complaint.

What this generator does not cover

These terms are for services provided by a business established in the UK. They do not suit the sale of goods or digital content, subscriptions and contracts that renew automatically, financial services, the specific rules of a regulated profession, or customers based outside the UK. Have your terms reviewed by a solicitor if your work carries significant risks.
