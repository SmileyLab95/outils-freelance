From letter before claim to judgment

  1. Letter before claim. To an individual or a sole trader, follow the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims: a Letter of Claim with the protocol's Information Sheet, Reply Form and Financial Statement, then 30 days for your client to reply. To a company, a letter with the amount, how you worked it out and a deadline: 14 days is reasonable in a straightforward case.
  2. Make your claim. Online on GOV.UK for a fixed amount under £100,000 against up to two defendants with an address in England or Wales. Pay the court fee: if you win, your client normally pays it back.
  3. Your client responds. They have 14 days after the claim is served to pay, admit or defend it, or 28 days if they first acknowledge the claim.
  4. No response? Ask the court for judgment as soon as the deadline has passed.
  5. A defence? For claims up to £10,000, both of you must first take part in a free one-hour mediation by phone with the Small Claims Mediation Service. Without an agreement, a judge decides the claim at a hearing, after you pay the hearing fee.
  6. Still not paid after judgment? Ask the court to enforce it: a warrant of control (enforcement agents), an attachment of earnings order (from wages), a charging order (on property) or a third party debt order (from a bank account). Each has its own court fee.

Good to know

  • Interest: between businesses, the Late Payment Act lets you add statutory interest and £40 to £100 of compensation per invoice: see the late payment interest calculator. For other debts, 8% a year is usual.
  • Keep your evidence: the contract or accepted quote, the invoice, your reminders and any reply from your client.
  • Time limit: 6 years from when payment was due, in England and Wales. Don't wait that long: the older the debt, the harder it is to recover.
