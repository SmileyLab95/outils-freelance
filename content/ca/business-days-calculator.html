Business days, statutory holidays and deadlines

  • Business days: the days you work, Monday to Friday by default, minus the statutory holidays you take off.
  • Statutory holidays: the general or public holidays listed by the employment standards law of each province or territory, or by the Canada Labour Code for federally regulated workplaces. Each date is computed from the rule the law sets: a fixed date, Easter, the third Monday in February, the first Monday in August or September, the second Monday in October, the Monday before May 25.
  • Calendar days: every day, weekends and statutory holidays included.

Holidays that fall on a weekend

Canada Day is July 2 when July 1 is a Sunday (federal Holidays Act), as Alberta’s official list and Quebec’s labour standards law also say; the calculator applies it where the law names Canada Day. Manitoba’s and Prince Edward Island’s laws name July 1 itself. In Quebec, when June 24 is a Sunday, June 25 is also a day off for employees who don’t normally work on Sundays. Other days off in lieu, when a holiday falls on a day off, depend on each law and employer: they are not counted.

Your billable days

To set your rate, start from the business days in the year, take off your vacation and the time you can't bill (finding clients, admin, learning): the hourly rate calculator does it for you.

What the calculator does not count

Statutory holidays are set for employees. Your clients, your bank and government offices may close on other days, such as a civic holiday in August in Ontario, or stay open on some of these: check their calendars. Saskatchewan, Yukon and Nunavut are not covered yet. A deadline set by a contract or by law is counted according to its own rules: check them for any deadline that affects your rights.

Laws read in November 2026, for the years 2024 to 2030. Thanksgiving Day: the second Monday in October, as in Quebec’s law and Alberta’s list (the federal date is set by proclamation). For information only.
