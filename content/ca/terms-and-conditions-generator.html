What these terms cover

  • For all your clients: who you are and how to reach you, how the contract is formed from your quote, prices in Canadian dollars with GST/HST, QST or PST added, payment and late interest written per month and per year, how you carry out the services, copyright and moral rights, liability, ending the contract, disputes and the law of your province.
  • With business clients: a period to check deliverables, the right to pause work on an unpaid invoice, a warranty disclaimer and a limit of liability that excludes intentional or gross fault and bodily or moral injury, and the courts of your province.
  • With consumers: the information their province requires before the contract, a copy by email within 15 days, a price that stays within 10% of an estimate, the right to cancel if the work has not started within 30 days, no cancellation fee or penalty, no limit of liability, no compulsory arbitration and no class action waiver.
  • In Quebec: the client may end the contract at any time and pays only for the work done (Civil Code of Québec, art. 2125 to 2129), and consumers served at a distance pay an advance only by credit card.

How to use them

  • Send your terms with every quote, as a PDF, before the client accepts it, and ask them to sign the quote or confirm their acceptance by email: this is what makes the terms part of the contract. Prepare your quotes with the invoice and estimate generator.
  • For consumers, put in each quote a detailed description of the services, the itemized price with taxes and other charges, the total, the payment terms and the start and end dates, then email them a copy of the accepted quote and of these terms within 15 days.
  • In Quebec, send the French version first (générateur de conditions générales).
  • Keep each dated version of your terms and a copy of every accepted quote. For a large project, add a freelance contract signed by both parties.

Working for consumers

Consumer protection laws apply when your client is an individual buying for personal, family or household purposes. The rules below come from the laws of Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia and Alberta, read on their official websites; the laws of other provinces and territories also protect consumers who live there.

  • Before the contract: give clear information they can keep and print, and let them correct errors and accept or decline (Ontario, above $50; Quebec; British Columbia; Alberta, above $50 for contracts made online).
  • After the contract: give them a written copy, within 15 days in Ontario (online contracts), Quebec and British Columbia. If the information or the copy is missing, they can cancel, usually within 7 days of receiving the copy, or longer without one.
  • Start on time: if you have not started within 30 days of the agreed date, they can cancel before you start (Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta).
  • Estimates: in Ontario, you cannot charge more than 10% above an estimate.
  • Terms you cannot use: compulsory arbitration (Ontario, Quebec, Alberta) or a class action waiver (Ontario, Quebec), a clause excluding your liability for your own acts, fees whose amount is not stated, and penalties other than interest (Quebec).

What this generator does not cover

These terms are for services provided by a business established in Canada. They do not cover contracts signed in person at a consumer’s home or away from your place of business (10-day cancellation period, official statement of rights and, in Quebec, an itinerant merchant permit), the sale of goods, subscriptions that renew automatically, personal development services such as fitness, credit, regulated professions, construction work, or clients based outside Canada. Have your terms reviewed by a lawyer (or a notary in Quebec) if your work carries significant risks. If a client does not pay, see the late fee calculator and the small claims court guide.
