    What to do

      The rule in one sentence

      A service supplied to a non-resident is generally zero-rated: the tax applies at 0%, but the sale remains a taxable supply. The exceptions mostly concern Canada itself: a service tied to real property or goods located in Canada, a service given to an individual in Canada, or litigation in Canada.

      Good to know

      • Exports count for the small supplier test: unlike in some countries, zero-rated sales are part of your worldwide taxable supplies. Once your taxable supplies, exports included, go over $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register.
      • Keep evidence of non-residence for each client: it supports the 0% rate.
      • Payment terms and currency are yours to agree: put them in your contract, together with who pays the bank fees.

      Information for guidance only, based on CRA memoranda 4.5.1 and 4.5.3, info sheet GI-034, guides RC4022 and RC4058, and Revenu Québec. For an unusual situation, check with the CRA, Revenu Québec or an accountant.
