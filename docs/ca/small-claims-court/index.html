How a small claim works

  1. Demand letter. Send a final written demand with the amount, a deadline and what you'll do next: the payment reminder generator writes it. In Québec, the formal demand (mise en demeure) is recommended and sometimes required.
  2. Choose the court. Usually in the province or territory where your client lives or does business, or where the events took place: each court's rules say where you can file.
  3. File your claim and pay the filing fee, which depends on the province and the amount. Some courts take claims online, such as Ontario's Small Claims Court Submissions Online portal and British Columbia's Civil Resolution Tribunal.
  4. Serve your client. The claim must reach your client in the way the court's rules require; your client then has a set time to file a defence.
  5. Settlement or mediation. Some courts hold a settlement conference or a mediation before the trial; it's mandatory in Québec for claims of $5,000 or less and in the Northwest Territories.
  6. Trial and judgment. A judge hears both sides and decides.
  7. Collect. If your client still doesn't pay, ask the court to enforce the judgment, for example by garnishing wages or a bank account, or by seizing property.

Good to know

  • Interest: claim the rate your contract provides, if any. The late fee calculator works it out.
  • Keep your evidence: the contract or accepted quote, the invoice, your reminders and any reply from your client.
  • Don't wait: in most provinces, you have only 2 years to sue.

Information for guidance only, from each court's official pages and each province's limitations law. For a large or complex claim, talk to a lawyer or your province's legal information service.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I sue a client for an unpaid invoice in Canada?
In the small claims court of a province or territory, usually where your client lives or does business, or where the events took place: each court's rules say where you can file. Each has its own limit, from $15,000 in Québec to $100,000 in Alberta.
What is the small claims limit in each province?
Ontario $50,000; Québec $15,000; British Columbia $35,000, with claims up to $5,000 going first to the Civil Resolution Tribunal; Alberta $100,000; Saskatchewan $50,000; Manitoba $20,000; Nova Scotia $25,000; New Brunswick $20,000; Prince Edward Island $16,000; Newfoundland and Labrador $25,000; Yukon $25,000; Northwest Territories $35,000. In most provinces, interest and costs don't count towards the limit.
Do I need a lawyer for small claims court?
No: small claims courts are designed for people who represent themselves. In Québec, the parties represent themselves, and a company is represented by an officer or an employee, not by a lawyer.
How long do I have to sue for an unpaid invoice?
Generally 2 years from when you knew, or ought to have known, about the claim in Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick; 3 years in Québec; 6 years in Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon and the Northwest Territories. Don't wait: count from the invoice due date to be safe.
What if my client owes more than the limit?
In most provinces, you can give up the amount above the limit to stay in small claims court, and you can't claim it later. Otherwise, sue for the full amount in a higher court, where a lawyer is usually worth it.
