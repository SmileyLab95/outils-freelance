Late payment interest: what you need to know

  • Agree on it first. Put the rate in your contract or quote and repeat it on every invoice.
  • State an annual rate. The Interest Act limits interest to 5% a year if a monthly rate is given without its annual equivalent.
  • Stay reasonable. The Criminal Code caps interest at 35% a year; 1% to 2% per month is common between businesses.

Information only, not legal advice. For large amounts, talk to a lawyer or a paralegal.

Frequently asked questions

Can I charge interest on a late invoice in Canada?
Yes, if your client agreed to it before the work, usually in your contract or quote. Unlike the UK or France, no law adds automatic interest or a fixed fee to late business invoices, so the clause is what makes it enforceable.
Why must the interest rate be stated per year?
Under section 4 of the federal Interest Act, if a contract states interest per month (or any period shorter than a year) without also stating the equivalent annual rate, you can only collect 5% a year. Write “1.5% per month (18% per year)”: the clause generated here does it for you.
What is a reasonable late fee?
1% to 2% per month (12% to 24% per year) is common in business contracts. The Criminal Code caps interest at a 35% annual percentage rate, and some provinces add consumer protection rules if your client is an individual.
How do I collect an unpaid invoice?
Send reminders with a firm deadline, then a demand letter. For smaller amounts, each province has a small claims court (Small Claims Court in Ontario, Civil Resolution Tribunal in BC, Court of Québec small claims division).
