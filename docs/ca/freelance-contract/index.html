What every freelance contract should cover

  • Scope and deliverables: the more precise, the fewer arguments about what's included.
  • Fees, taxes, deposit and payment terms: say that GST/HST is extra, ask for a deposit and state a yearly interest rate for late payments. Chase late invoices with a payment reminder.
  • Ownership: assign the copyright, and waive moral rights, only once you're paid in full.
  • Termination: notice, and payment for the work already done.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a written contract as a freelancer?
It isn't required by law, but it's the best way to agree scope, payment and ownership before problems arise. It also documents that you're self-employed, which matters to the CRA and Revenu Québec when they look at your status.
Who owns the work: me or my client?
As a contractor, you're the first owner of the copyright in what you create, unless you assign it in writing. Moral rights can't be assigned, only waived. This template assigns the copyright and waives moral rights once you're paid in full.
Can I charge interest on late payments?
Yes, if your contract or invoices say so. The Interest Act requires the rate to be stated as a yearly rate: “2% per month” alone isn't enough. See the late fee calculator.
What changes in Quebec?
Under the Civil Code of Québec, a client can end a contract for services at any time by paying for the work done and the expenses incurred, while the contractor can only end it for a serious reason. The template applies these rules when you choose Quebec law.
Is this contract legally binding?
Yes, once both parties sign it, including with an electronic signature. It is a general template for business-to-business projects, not legal advice: for a large project, work abroad or sensitive data, have a lawyer review it.
