    What a winning proposal covers

    • The client's situation, in their words: show that you understood the need before talking about yourself.
    • What you propose: the approach and the result, not a list of technologies.
    • Steps, timing and fees: a clear table, a total, the deposit and the payment terms.
    • Optional extras: they let the client adjust the budget without reopening the whole project.
    • Next steps and a validity date: the client knows what to do and by when.

    Frequently asked questions

    What's the difference between a proposal and an estimate?
    A proposal explains the client's need, your approach, the steps and the price: it is there to win the project and agree on the scope. An estimate lists the work and the price. Once the client says yes, send the freelance contract and the deposit invoice from the invoice generator.
    Is the proposal binding?
    The template says so plainly: it is not a contract, and you will both be bound once the contract is signed. Until then, the scope and the price can still be discussed; the contract then sets the terms.
    Why add a validity date?
    It tells the client how long your price and your availability hold, and gives a natural reason to decide. After that date, update the proposal before accepting the project.
    Do I add GST/HST?
    The proposal states that fees are exclusive of GST/HST and, where applicable, QST or PST, which will be added at the applicable rates. The GST/HST calculator gives the rate for your client's province.
    How do I price each step?
    Estimate the time each task takes and add a margin for the unexpected with the project price calculator, starting from a rate worked out with the rate calculator. One or two optional extras, such as maintenance or training, let the client adjust the budget: they are shown apart and are not counted in the fee.
