    How the conversion works

    A daily rate is the hourly rate times the hours you bill in a day. A week is 5 billed days. The yearly figure is the daily rate times the days you bill in a year, and a month is a twelfth of it: an average, since some months have more time off than others.

    The number of billed days is what turns a rate into a yearly income. After 4 weeks off and 10 holidays, a year has about 230 working days, but part of that time goes to finding clients, admin and training, which you can't bill. Enter your own estimate.

    To work out what to charge for the income you want, use the hourly rate calculator; to see how much of each payment to save for taxes, the set-aside calculator.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I convert an hourly rate to a yearly income?
    Multiply the hourly rate by the hours you bill in a day, then by the days you bill in a year. With 8 hours a day and 161 billed days (1,288 hours), $100 an hour is $128,800 a year, before expenses and taxes.
    How many hours does a freelancer bill per year?
    Fewer than an employee is paid for. The default here is 1,288 hours: 52 weeks minus 4 weeks off and 10 holidays, 40 hours a week, and 30% of that time spent on unbillable work such as sales, admin and training. Change it to match your year, or work it out with the hourly rate calculator.
    Why not just multiply by 2,080 hours?
    2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks) is a full-time employee's paid year, holidays included. A freelancer isn't paid for time off or for the hours spent finding clients and running the business, so the hours you bill are fewer.
    Is the yearly figure what I earn?
    No: it's what you bill. Business expenses, income tax and CPP contributions (QPP in Quebec) come out of it. To see what to keep aside for taxes, use the set-aside calculator.
