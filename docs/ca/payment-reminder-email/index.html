    A simple follow-up schedule

    1. On the due date: a friendly reminder with the invoice attached.
    2. A week later: a second reminder asking when payment will be made. A phone call helps.
    3. Three weeks late: a firm reminder with a 7-day deadline and your interest clause.
    4. After about 45 days: a demand letter with a final deadline, then small claims court if needed.

    Frequently asked questions

    When should I send a payment reminder?
    A friendly reminder on or just after the due date, a second reminder about a week later, a firm reminder after three weeks, and a demand letter after about 45 days. Most late invoices are paid after the first or second reminder.
    What is a demand letter?
    A formal letter stating the amount owed, a final deadline (often 10 days) and what you will do next, usually a claim in small claims court. In Québec, it is called a “mise en demeure” and is generally expected before going to court.
    Can I add interest to a late invoice?
    Only if your client agreed to it, typically in your contract or quote, and the rate must be expressed as an annual rate under the Interest Act. Work out the amount with our late payment interest calculator.
    Where do I sue for an unpaid invoice?
    Each province has a small claims court with its own limit, such as the Small Claims Court in Ontario, the Civil Resolution Tribunal in British Columbia, and the small claims division of the Court of Québec. The small claims court finder gives the court, its limit and the time limit to sue in each province and territory.
