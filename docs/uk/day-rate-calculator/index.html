The maths

Day rate = (profit needed + expenses) ÷ billable days × (1 + safety margin)

The profit needed is found exactly with 2026/27 income tax and Class 4 National Insurance, so the rate stays right in every tax band, including the tapering of the Personal Allowance above £100,000.

Billable days start from 260 weekdays, minus your holidays and bank holidays, minus the share of time you cannot bill.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out my freelance day rate?
Add the take-home pay you want, the income tax and National Insurance on your profit, and your business expenses, then divide by the days you can actually bill in a year. This calculator does it with 2026/27 sole trader rates.
How many days a year can a freelancer bill?
There are about 260 weekdays a year. Take off 8 bank holidays, your holidays and sick days, and the time spent finding work, admin and training: most freelancers bill between 160 and 200 days a year.
How do I convert a salary into a day rate?
A common shortcut is to divide the equivalent annual salary by about 200 and add 20 to 50%, because a freelancer pays for holidays, pension, equipment and quiet periods. The calculator gives a more precise answer based on your own figures.
Should contractors inside IR35 use this calculator?
No. Inside IR35, your client or an umbrella company deducts tax and National Insurance like a salary, and employer costs may be taken from your rate. This calculator assumes you are self-employed outside IR35.
