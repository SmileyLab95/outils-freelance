A simple system that works

  1. Open a separate savings account just for tax, and VAT if you're registered.
  2. Every time a client pays you, move your share to it the same day. Don't wait for the end of the month.
  3. Pay HMRC from that account: your balancing payment and first payment on account on 31 January, the second payment on account on 31 July. The tax calendar reminds you of each date.
  4. Recalculate every few months as your income changes: the share rises with your profit.

Your first year

Payments on account are based on the previous year's bill, so HMRC usually asks for nothing during your first tax year. Then, on 31 January, you pay the whole year's bill and the first payment on account for the next year: half the bill again, unless the bill is under £1,000 or 80% or more of your tax was taken at source. Start saving from your very first payment.

VAT

You must register for VAT when your taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in any rolling 12-month period. Once registered, the VAT you charge is not your money: keep all of it aside for your VAT return. You can usually reclaim the VAT on purchases you use for your taxable business supplies, with a valid VAT invoice, so keeping the full amount is the cautious option. The VAT calculator adds or removes VAT from any price.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage should a sole trader set aside for tax?
It depends on your profit, where you live and any other income. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, income tax is 20% above the £12,570 Personal Allowance and 40% above £50,270 (Scotland has its own bands); Class 4 National Insurance is 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2% above. With no other income, that's about 15.1% of a £30,000 profit, 19.5% of £50,000 and 30.7% of £100,000. The calculator works out your own percentage from the 2026/27 rates.
Should I set aside VAT too?
Yes, if you're VAT-registered: the VAT in each payment belongs to HMRC and is paid with your VAT return. At the 20% standard rate, that's one sixth of a VAT-inclusive payment. The calculator takes it out first, then works out the tax share on the rest. It doesn't cover the Flat Rate Scheme, where you pay a fixed percentage of your VAT-inclusive turnover instead.
I also have a job: how much should I save?
Enter your salary. Your employer already takes income tax on it through PAYE, but your profit is added on top of your salary, so it's taxed at the rates above it: you may need to save a bigger share. Class 4 National Insurance only applies to your profit.
Why is my first tax bill so big?
Payments on account are based on the previous year's bill, so in your first tax year HMRC usually asks for nothing until the 31 January after the tax year ends. On that date you pay the whole year's tax plus the first payment on account for the next year, half of your bill, unless the bill is under £1,000 or 80% or more of your tax was taken at source. Saving from your first payment avoids the shock.
Is this the exact amount I'll owe?
It's an estimate for planning. It doesn't include Student Loan repayments, the High Income Child Benefit Charge, savings or dividend income, tax reliefs, or the National Insurance annual maximum if you're also employed (HMRC can refund Class 4 paid above it). For the detailed breakdown, use the self-employed tax calculator.
