    How the calculator works

    • Break-even work loss: 1 minus your current rate divided by your new rate. Lose less work than that and you earn more than today; lose more and you earn less.
    • Inflation: enter the latest 12-month figure from the Office for National Statistics to see the rate that keeps pace, and your real increase after inflation.

    Before you announce it

    • Check your numbers with the rate calculator, which starts from the income you need.
    • Tell your clients in writing, ahead of time, with the date and the new rate; keep work already agreed at today's rate. Copy the rate increase email template.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much should I raise my rates?
    There's no rule: many freelancers review their rates once a year. This calculator shows what a given increase earns you and how much work you could lose before earning less than today.
    How much work can I lose after a rate increase?
    With a 10% increase, you can lose up to 9.1% of your billed time before your monthly revenue falls below today's: 1 minus the old rate divided by the new rate.
    Should my rates keep up with inflation?
    If your costs and your clients' prices rise, a rate that stays the same is worth less each year. Enter the latest inflation figure from the official statistics agency to see the rate that keeps pace and your real increase.
    How do I announce a rate increase?
    Tell your clients in writing, ahead of time, with the date and the new rate, and keep work already agreed at the current rate. The email templates page has a ready-to-copy message.
