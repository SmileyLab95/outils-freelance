    VAT for freelancers and sole traders

    • Registration threshold: £90,000 of VAT taxable turnover over any rolling 12 months. Below it, you don't charge VAT unless you register voluntarily.
    • Invoices: once registered, your invoices must show your VAT number, the VAT rate and the VAT amount. A simplified VAT invoice is allowed for supplies up to £250. Our invoice generator adds the VAT line for you.
    • Flat Rate Scheme: for turnover up to £150,000, pay HMRC a fixed percentage of your gross turnover; the limited cost trader rate is 16.5%.

    Information only. Check the VAT treatment of your goods or services on GOV.UK.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I add VAT to a price?
    Multiply the net price by 1.2 for the 20% standard rate. For example, £500 + VAT = £500 × 1.2 = £600, of which £100 is VAT. At the 5% reduced rate, multiply by 1.05.
    How do I remove VAT from a price?
    Divide the gross price by 1.2 (at 20%). £600 ÷ 1.2 = £500 net, so the VAT is £100. Taking 20% off £600 would give £480, which is wrong: that's the most common VAT mistake.
    When do I have to register for VAT?
    When your VAT taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in any rolling 12-month period, or if you expect it to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. You can deregister if it drops below £88,000. You can also register voluntarily below the threshold.
    What is the VAT Flat Rate Scheme?
    A simpler scheme for businesses with taxable turnover up to £150,000: you charge clients VAT as usual but pay HMRC a fixed percentage of your gross turnover. If you spend very little on goods, the limited cost trader rate of 16.5% applies, which often removes the benefit.
