Invoicing overseas clients

Tell us where your client is, whether they're a business or a private person, and what you sell: find out whether to charge UK VAT, what to put on the invoice and what to do next.

Your invoice

A business belongs where it's established; a private client, where they live. The Isle of Man counts as the UK for VAT; the Channel Islands and Gibraltar don't.
Consultancy covers consultants, engineers, lawyers, accountants and similar services; licensing your copyright counts too. Digital services are supplied over the internet with little or no human involvement, such as pre-recorded courses.

For services only: selling goods follows other rules.

    What to do

      The rule in one sentence

      For a service, VAT generally follows the client's country when the client is a business, and your country when the client is a private person, with exceptions for consultancy-type services to private clients outside the UK, digital services and services tied to a place (property, events, transport).

      Good to know

      • Check that your client is a business before leaving VAT off: a VAT number, a company registration or their website. Without evidence, the private-client rules apply.
      • Digital services to EU consumers have no threshold for UK businesses: EU VAT applies from the first sale.
      • Flat Rate Scheme: how these sales count in your flat rate turnover isn't covered here; check VAT Notice 733.
      • Payment terms and currency are yours to agree: put them in your contract, together with who pays the bank fees.

      US clients: Form W-8BEN

      A client in the United States will often ask you for a Form W-8BEN. You give it to your client, not to the IRS: it shows you're not a US person. Fill it in before the first payment.

      • Work done from the UK: pay to a non-US resident for work done outside the United States isn't US-source income, so there's no US withholding on it.
      • Without the form, your client may have to withhold 30% of your payments.
      • Who fills it in: an individual, sole traders included; a company uses Form W-8BEN-E.
      • How long it lasts: until 31 December of the third year after you sign it (a form signed in 2026 lasts until the end of 2029), unless your circumstances change, which calls for a new form.
      • Work done in the United States: that part is US-source income, and an exemption is claimed on a different form (Form 8233).

      Information for guidance only, based on HMRC's VAT Notices 741A, 700/1 and 700/12 and its place of supply manual, and for Form W-8BEN on the IRS instructions and Publication 515. For an unusual situation, check with HMRC or an accountant.

      Frequently asked questions

      Do I charge VAT to a client outside the UK?
      Not if your client is a business: a service to a business is supplied where the business belongs, so it's outside the scope of UK VAT, unless a special rule applies (property, events, transport). For a private client, it depends on the service: consultancy and similar professional services, advertising, data processing and information supplied to someone who belongs outside the UK are also outside the scope; services that follow the general rule are taxed in the UK, as for a UK client.
      What do I write on an invoice to a business client abroad?
      No UK VAT. HMRC doesn't set a wording for these invoices: a clear statement such as “Outside the scope of UK VAT” helps your client, who accounts for any VAT in its own country. For an EU client, add its VAT number: it's the best evidence that it's a business. In the invoice generator, choose “No UK VAT (outside the scope)”.
      I sell pre-recorded online courses or software to consumers in the EU: what changes?
      Automated digital services to consumers are supplied where the consumer belongs. For a UK business, EU VAT is due on every sale to an EU consumer, whatever the amount, even if you're not VAT-registered in the UK: register for the EU's non-Union scheme in one EU country, or for VAT in each country where you have customers.
      Do overseas sales count towards the VAT registration threshold?
      Sales outside the scope of UK VAT aren't taxable supplies made in the UK, so they don't count towards the £90,000 threshold. If you're VAT-registered, you still include them in box 6 of your VAT Return. A UK business that only supplies customers outside the UK can register voluntarily.
      Can I invoice in euros or dollars?
      Yes, the amounts can be in your client's currency. A VAT invoice must still show the total VAT in sterling: that matters only when you charge UK VAT.
      A US client asked me for a W-8BEN: what do I do?
      Fill it in and give it to your client (not the IRS) before the first payment: it shows you're not a US person. For work done from the UK, your pay isn't US-source income, so there's no US withholding; without the form, your client may have to withhold 30%. A company uses Form W-8BEN-E.
