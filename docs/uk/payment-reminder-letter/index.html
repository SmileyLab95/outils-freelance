    From reminder to small claim

    1. On the due date: a friendly reminder with the invoice attached.
    2. A week later: a second reminder asking when payment will be made. A phone call helps.
    3. Three weeks late: a final reminder with a 7-day deadline and statutory interest.
    4. After about 45 days: a letter before action with a clear deadline (30 days if your client is an individual or sole trader).
    5. Still unpaid: a court claim, online for fixed amounts under £100,000: the small claims calculator gives the court fee and every step.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I write a polite payment reminder?
    Keep it short: the invoice number, amount and due date, the invoice attached, and a simple question about when you can expect payment. Assume it was an oversight; most late invoices are paid after the first or second reminder.
    What is a letter before action?
    A formal letter warning that you will start court proceedings if the debt is not paid by a deadline. It is the last step before a small claim through the County Court (Money Claim Online). If your client is an individual or a sole trader, the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims asks you to give them 30 days to respond and to include certain information.
    Can I add interest to my reminder?
    Yes, if your client is a business. The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 lets you claim statutory interest (8% plus the Bank of England base rate) and fixed compensation of £40 to £100 per invoice. Work out the amounts with our late payment interest calculator.
    When should I send each reminder?
    A friendly reminder on or just after the due date, a second reminder after about a week, a final reminder mentioning statutory interest after three weeks, and a letter before action after about 45 days.
