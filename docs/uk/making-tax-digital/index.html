What changes with Making Tax Digital

  1. Digital records: record each income and expense in MTD-compatible software (or a spreadsheet linked to bridging software).
  2. Quarterly updates: send a summary of your income and expenses to HMRC every three months.
  3. Final declaration: confirm your figures and add other income by 31 January after the tax year, instead of the usual return.

Your tax bill and payment dates don't change: you still pay by 31 January and, if needed, make payments on account on 31 January and 31 July.

Frequently asked questions

Who has to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?
Sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income (gross self-employment turnover plus gross property income, before expenses) is over £50,000 must use it from 6 April 2026, based on their 2024/25 return. The threshold falls to £30,000 from April 2027 and is planned to fall to £20,000 from April 2028.
What do I have to do under MTD?
Keep digital records of your income and expenses in MTD-compatible software, send a quarterly update to HMRC through that software, and submit a final tax return by 31 January after the end of the tax year. Your tax is still paid on the usual dates.
What are the quarterly update deadlines?
For the 2026/27 tax year: 7 August 2026, 7 November 2026, 7 February 2027 and 7 May 2027, covering the quarters ending 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April.
Is there a penalty for late quarterly updates?
MTD uses a points system: each late submission adds a point, and a £200 penalty applies once you reach the threshold (4 points for quarterly updates). HMRC has said it will not give points for late quarterly updates in 2026/27, the first year.
