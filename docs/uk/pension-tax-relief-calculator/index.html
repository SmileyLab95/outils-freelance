Frequently asked questions

How does pension tax relief work for the self-employed?
You pay into a personal pension or SIPP from your taxed income, and your provider adds 20% basic-rate relief: £800 paid in becomes £1,000. If you pay higher or additional rate tax, you claim the rest of the relief in your Self Assessment return, which lowers your tax bill.
How much can I pay in and get tax relief?
Up to 100% of your relevant UK earnings (your trading profit for a sole trader), or £3,600 gross if you earn less, and within the £60,000 annual allowance. You can carry forward unused allowance from the previous three tax years if you were a member of a pension scheme.
Do pension contributions reduce National Insurance?
Not for the self-employed: Class 4 National Insurance is calculated on your profits before pension contributions. A limited company director can save National Insurance through employer contributions, but a sole trader can't.
Why is my relief higher than 40%?
Between £100,000 and £125,140 of income, you lose £1 of personal allowance for every £2 earned. A pension contribution reduces your adjusted net income, which gives the allowance back: the effective relief in that band reaches 60%.
When should I claim higher-rate relief?
In your Self Assessment return for the tax year of the payment, in the section on pension contributions: enter the gross amount (what you paid plus the 20% added by the provider).
