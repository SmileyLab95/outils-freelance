From letter before claim to judgment

  1. Letter before claim. To an individual or a sole trader, follow the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims: a Letter of Claim with the protocol's Information Sheet, Reply Form and Financial Statement, then 30 days for your client to reply. To a company, a letter with the amount, how you worked it out and a deadline: 14 days is reasonable in a straightforward case.
  2. Make your claim. Online on GOV.UK for a fixed amount under £100,000 against up to two defendants with an address in England or Wales. Pay the court fee: if you win, your client normally pays it back.
  3. Your client responds. They have 14 days after the claim is served to pay, admit or defend it, or 28 days if they first acknowledge the claim.
  4. No response? Ask the court for judgment as soon as the deadline has passed.
  5. A defence? For claims up to £10,000, both of you must first take part in a free one-hour mediation by phone with the Small Claims Mediation Service. Without an agreement, a judge decides the claim at a hearing, after you pay the hearing fee.
  6. Still not paid after judgment? Ask the court to enforce it: a warrant of control (enforcement agents), an attachment of earnings order (from wages), a charging order (on property) or a third party debt order (from a bank account). Each has its own court fee.

Good to know

  • Interest: between businesses, the Late Payment Act lets you add statutory interest and £40 to £100 of compensation per invoice: see the late payment interest calculator. For other debts, 8% a year is usual.
  • Keep your evidence: the contract or accepted quote, the invoice, your reminders and any reply from your client.
  • Time limit: 6 years from when payment was due, in England and Wales. Don't wait that long: the older the debt, the harder it is to recover.

Information for guidance only, based on GOV.UK, the Civil Procedure Rules and their pre-action protocols, and the Limitation Act 1980. For a large or disputed claim, get advice from a solicitor or Citizens Advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take a client to court for an unpaid invoice?
Yes. In England and Wales, you can make a money claim in the County Court, online for fixed amounts under £100,000. Claims of £10,000 or less, not counting interest, normally go to the small claims track, designed for people who don't use a solicitor. Send a letter before claim first: the court expects it.
How much does it cost to make a court claim for money?
The court fee depends on the amount you claim, interest included: £35 up to £300, £115 from £1,500.01 to £3,000, £455 from £5,000.01 to £10,000, then 5% of the claim up to £200,000. If your client defends the claim and it goes to a small claims hearing, there's a hearing fee of £27 to £346. If you win, your client normally pays the court fees back.
Do I need a solicitor for a small claim?
No. The small claims track is designed for people representing themselves, and each side usually pays its own legal costs, so a solicitor's fees are rarely recovered. Above £10,000, legal costs and hearing fees are higher: legal advice is worth getting.
How long do I have to claim an unpaid invoice?
In England and Wales, 6 years from the date your right to sue arose, usually when payment was due (Limitation Act 1980). A part payment, or an acknowledgment of the debt in writing and signed by your client, restarts the clock. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own time limits.
What happens if my client ignores the claim?
Your client has 14 days after the claim is served to respond, or 28 days if they acknowledge it first. If they don't respond in time, you can ask the court for judgment. If they still don't pay, you can ask the court to enforce the judgment, for example by sending enforcement agents or taking money from their wages.
