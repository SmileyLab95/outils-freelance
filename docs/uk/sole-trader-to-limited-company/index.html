    Before you switch

    A limited company is a separate legal entity: it has its own money, its own tax return and its own obligations, and you're paid through a salary or dividends. Check that it's worth it with the sole trader vs limited company comparison, and ask an accountant how to move your business's assets, contracts and clients to the company.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I change from sole trader to limited company?
    Register the company with Companies House (£100 online), register it for Corporation Tax within 3 months of starting to do business, tell HMRC you've stopped being self-employed and send your last Self Assessment return. If the company pays you a salary, register it as an employer before the first payday.
    Can I keep my VAT number?
    Yes, you can ask HMRC to transfer it with form VAT68, sent with a new VAT registration, before the old registration is cancelled. The company then takes on any VAT you still owe as a sole trader. Otherwise, cancel your registration and register the company.
    Do I need a new bank account?
    Yes. A limited company is a separate legal entity: its money must stay separate from yours, in an account in the company's name.
    Does my company need employers' liability insurance?
    As soon as it employs anyone else, yes: at least £5 million of cover. If you're the company's only worker, check the exemptions in the GOV.UK and HSE guidance.
