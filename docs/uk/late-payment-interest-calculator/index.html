How statutory interest works

Interest = amount × (8% + reference rate) × days late ÷ 365

The reference rate is the Bank of England base rate on 31 December (for debts that became overdue between 1 January and 30 June) or on 30 June (for debts overdue between 1 July and 31 December). It stays fixed for that debt, even if the base rate changes later.

  • Debts overdue in the first half of 2025: 4.75% + 8% = 12.75%.
  • Debts overdue in the second half of 2025: 4.25% + 8% = 12.25%.
  • Debts overdue in 2026: 3.75% + 8% = 11.75%.

Interest runs until the debt is paid. Claiming it is your right, but many freelancers use it as leverage: mention it in your reminder and waive it if the client pays promptly.

Frequently asked questions

Can I charge interest on a late invoice in the UK?
Yes. Under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, a business can claim statutory interest on late payments from another business, even if the contract says nothing about it, unless you agreed a different, substantial remedy in your contract.
What is the statutory interest rate?
8% a year plus the Bank of England base rate on the reference date: 31 December for debts that became overdue in January to June, 30 June for July to December. For debts overdue in 2026, the base rate was 3.75% on both dates, so the rate is 11.75% a year.
What is the fixed compensation for late payment?
On top of interest, you can claim a fixed sum for each late invoice: £40 for debts under £1,000, £70 from £1,000 to £9,999.99, and £100 for £10,000 or more. You can also claim reasonable recovery costs above that.
When is a payment late if we didn't agree a date?
30 days after the customer received your invoice or the goods or services, whichever is later. Between businesses, agreed payment terms can be up to 60 days unless both sides agree longer terms that are not grossly unfair.
