Frequently asked questions

What must a sole trader invoice include?
A unique invoice number, your name (and any trading name), an address where documents can be sent to you, your client's name and address, the invoice date, a description of what you supplied, the date of supply, the amount, and the total. If you are VAT registered, you also need your VAT number, the VAT rate and the VAT amount.
What extra details does a limited company invoice need?
The full registered company name, exactly as on the certificate of incorporation. The company registration number, the place of registration (for example England and Wales) and the registered office address are required on business letters, order forms and websites, so add them to invoices too, in the “Company details” field. The invoice checklist lists every detail.
Do I add VAT if I'm not VAT registered?
No. If you are not registered for VAT, you must not charge it or show a VAT amount. Choose “Not VAT registered” in the form. Registration becomes compulsory when your taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months.
What payment terms should I use?
If you don't agree payment terms, the law treats payment as late 30 days after the invoice. Shorter terms (7 or 14 days) are common for freelancers. State them clearly on the invoice, together with your bank details.
How do I save the invoice as a PDF?
