How the comparison works

On the employee side, the calculator starts from your salary and takes off Class 1 National Insurance and Income Tax (Scottish rates if you live in Scotland), with the personal allowance tapered above £100,000. Your employer's National Insurance and pension contributions are paid on top of your salary: they aren't part of your income, unless you add the pension to compare like with like.

On the freelance side, it looks for the turnover that leaves the same income as a sole trader, after your expenses, Class 4 National Insurance and Income Tax on your profit. Divided by your billed days, it gives the equivalent day rate. The self-employed tax calculator shows the full detail, and the sole trader vs limited company comparison what changes with a company.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to earn as a freelancer to replace my salary?
With a £40,000 salary, you keep £32,320 after Income Tax and National Insurance (outside Scotland). As a sole trader with no expenses, you need £39,259 in turnover to keep the same amount, or £215.71 a day over 182 billed days.
Why does my day rate need to be higher than my daily salary?
Because you only bill part of your working days: holidays, bank holidays, finding clients and admin aren't paid. You also pay for your own equipment, insurance and pension. The calculator divides the turnover you need by the days you actually bill (182 by default, the assumption of the day rate calculator).
Is my employer's pension contribution included?
Only if you enter it. If you're eligible for automatic enrolment, your employer must pay a minimum into your workplace pension: the calculator shows that minimum for your salary, and you can add it, or what your employer really pays, under “Employer pension and benefits”.
Why can a sole trader need less than the salary?
Self-employed people pay Class 4 National Insurance at a lower rate than employees' Class 1, on profits above the same threshold, and the same Income Tax. That's why, with no expenses, the turnover needed can be close to the salary or below it. But holidays, sick days and gaps between contracts aren't paid: the day rate is what really matters.
