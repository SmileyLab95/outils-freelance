Sole trader: what to know before you start

As a sole trader, you run your business as an individual: you keep all the profits after tax, and you're personally responsible for any losses the business makes. You pay Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance on your profits through Self Assessment.

Before you start, compare with a limited company in the sole trader vs limited company comparison, estimate your tax with the self-employed tax calculator and set your price with the day rate calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I become a sole trader?
You can start trading straight away. You register as a sole trader by registering for Self Assessment with HMRC, which you must do once your trading income goes over £1,000 in a tax year, by 5 October after the end of that tax year. You'll need your National Insurance number.
When do I have to register as self-employed?
By 5 October after the end of the tax year (6 April to 5 April) in which your trading income first went over £1,000. For a business started in the 2026/27 tax year, that's 5 October 2027. Registering late can lead to a penalty.
Do I need to register a business name?
No. If you trade under a name other than your own, it can't include 'limited', 'Ltd', 'LLP' or 'plc', can't be offensive or the same as an existing trade mark, and some sensitive words need permission. Registering a trade mark is the way to stop others using it.
How long do I keep my records?
For at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the tax year they relate to: keep records of all your business income and expenses.
Do I need to pay the ICO data protection fee?
If you use personal information, such as your customers' details, you may have to, unless you're exempt (for example if you only use it for your own accounts, staff administration or marketing your own services). The ICO's self-assessment tool tells you.
