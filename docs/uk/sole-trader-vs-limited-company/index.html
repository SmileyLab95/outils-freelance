How the comparison works

  • Sole trader: income tax and Class 4 National Insurance on the whole profit.
  • Limited company: you are the only director and employee. The company pays your salary and 15% employer National Insurance above £5,000, then corporation tax (19% up to £50,000, 25% from £250,000, with marginal relief in between) on what is left. The rest is paid to you as dividends, taxed at 10.75%, 35.75% or 39.35% after the £500 dividend allowance.

Real life is rarely that tidy: you may keep profits in the company, contribute to a pension through it, or have other income. Use this as a first estimate, then confirm with an accountant before you incorporate.

Contractors: check IR35

If you work through your own company for a client in a way that looks like employment, the off-payroll working rules (IR35) can tax your income much like a salary, removing most of the advantage of a limited company.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to be a sole trader or a limited company in 2026?
It depends on your profit. With corporation tax at 19% to 25%, employer National Insurance at 15% above £5,000 and higher dividend tax rates from April 2026 (10.75% and 35.75%), the tax saving of a limited company has shrunk. Below roughly £50,000 of profit the difference is often small, and the extra accounting costs can wipe it out. Run your numbers with the calculator.
What salary should a limited company director pay themselves?
Most single directors pay a salary around the Personal Allowance (£12,570) or the employer National Insurance threshold (£5,000), then take the rest as dividends. A salary of at least £6,708 (the lower earnings limit) protects your State Pension record. The calculator lets you compare both.
Can I claim the Employment Allowance as a sole director?
No. A company whose only employee paid above the secondary threshold is a director cannot claim the £10,500 Employment Allowance. The calculator assumes no allowance.
What else should I consider besides tax?
A limited company gives you limited liability and can look more established to some clients, but it means more admin: annual accounts, a confirmation statement, a corporation tax return, payroll, and usually an accountant. Contractors should also check IR35, which can tax an outside-IR35 company much like an employee.
