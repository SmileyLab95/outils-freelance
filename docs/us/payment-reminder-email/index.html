A simple follow-up schedule that works

  1. Before or on the due date: a friendly reminder with the invoice attached.
  2. About a week late: a second reminder asking when payment will be made.
  3. Three weeks late: a firm reminder with a 7-day deadline, your late fee and, where it applies, the freelancer protection law.
  4. After 45 days: a final notice with a deadline and the next step (small claims court or a collection agency).

Between emails, a phone call often works faster. And to avoid the problem next time, ask for a deposit and put your payment terms in writing.

Frequently asked questions

When should I send a payment reminder?
A friendly reminder a few days before or right after the due date, a second reminder about a week later, a firm reminder after three weeks, and a final notice after about 45 days. Most late invoices are paid after the first or second reminder.
How do I politely ask a client for payment?
Keep it short and factual: the invoice number, the amount, the due date, the invoice attached, and a simple question about when payment will be made. Assume good faith in the first reminders; invoices often get lost or stuck in approval.
What should a final notice say?
That the invoice is still unpaid despite previous reminders, the amount owed, a clear deadline (7 to 10 days), and what you will do next, such as filing in small claims court or using a collection agency. Only state actions you are ready to take.
What laws protect freelancers from late payment?
New York City and New York State (Freelance Isn't Free Acts), Illinois (Freelance Worker Protection Act) and California (SB 988) require written contracts above a threshold and payment by the agreed date, or within 30 days of completing the work if no date was set. Several cities have similar rules.
