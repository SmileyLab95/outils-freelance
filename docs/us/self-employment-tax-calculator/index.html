      How self-employment tax is calculated

      As a freelancer, independent contractor or sole proprietor, you pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare yourself. The IRS applies the rate to 92.35% of your net profit, which mirrors the employer half being deductible for a business.

      SE tax = net profit × 92.35% × 15.3%　(12.4% Social Security up to $184,500 + 2.9% Medicare)

      Then federal income tax is calculated on your adjusted gross income, after the deduction for half of your self-employment tax, the standard deduction and the qualified business income (QBI) deduction.

      Frequently asked questions

      How much is self-employment tax in 2026?
      15.3% of your net earnings from self-employment: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare. Net earnings are 92.35% of your net profit. The Social Security part stops at the 2026 wage base of $184,500 (including any W-2 wages); the Medicare part has no limit, and an extra 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies above $200,000 ($250,000 for married couples filing jointly).
      Do freelancers pay both self-employment tax and income tax?
      Yes. Self-employment tax replaces the Social Security and Medicare taxes an employer would withhold, and federal income tax is due on top of it. Half of your self-employment tax is deductible when computing your income tax, which this calculator applies automatically.
      What is the QBI deduction?
      The qualified business income deduction (Section 199A) lets most sole proprietors deduct up to 20% of their business profit, limited to 20% of taxable income. It was made permanent in 2025. Above $201,750 of taxable income ($403,500 married filing jointly) it phases out for service businesses such as consulting, law, accounting and health, and for businesses without employees.
      How much should I set aside for taxes as a freelancer?
      Use the percentage shown by the calculator: it is your total tax divided by your profit. For a single filer with no state income tax, it's about 19.5% of a $50,000 profit and 22.4% of $100,000; it rises with income and state tax. Put that share of every payment into a separate savings account so the money is there when quarterly payments are due.
      Do I need to pay quarterly estimated taxes?
      If you expect to owe at least $1,000 in federal tax for the year after withholding, yes. Payments for 2026 are due April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Our quarterly tax calculator shows the minimum that avoids an underpayment penalty.
      Does this calculator include state taxes?
      Only as a rough estimate: enter your state's approximate income tax rate. Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming have no individual income tax on wages and business income. Local taxes, tax credits, retirement contributions and the self-employed health insurance deduction are not included.
