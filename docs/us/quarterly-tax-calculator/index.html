How the minimum is calculated

The IRS does not penalize you if, by each due date, your withholding and estimated payments add up to a quarter of the required annual payment. That amount is the smaller of:

  • 90% of the tax you will owe for 2026, or
  • 100% of the total tax on your 2025 return (110% if your 2025 adjusted gross income was over $150,000), provided you filed a 2025 return covering 12 months.

Paying the prior-year amount is the safest choice when your income grows: you know the number in advance and avoid penalties, even if you owe more when you file. If your income drops, 90% of this year's tax may be lower.

Frequently asked questions

When are quarterly estimated taxes due for 2026?
April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027. You can skip the January payment if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay all the tax due with it.
Who has to pay estimated taxes?
Generally anyone who expects to owe at least $1,000 in federal tax for the year after subtracting withholding and refundable credits. That includes most freelancers, independent contractors and sole proprietors.
What is the safe harbor rule?
You avoid the underpayment penalty if your withholding plus timely estimated payments cover at least the smaller of: 90% of this year's tax, or 100% of last year's tax. If last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately), the prior-year figure becomes 110%.
What happens if I pay less than required?
The IRS charges an underpayment penalty, calculated like interest on the amount that was short and for how long. Paying on time, even partially, reduces it. You still owe the full tax when you file.
How do I pay quarterly taxes?
Online with IRS Direct Pay or your IRS Online Account, through EFTPS, by debit or credit card, or by mailing a check with a Form 1040-ES voucher. Your state may have its own estimated tax payments.
