How to use these laws when a client pays late

  1. Before the work: send a written contract with the required details. Under the New York and Illinois laws, a client who refuses to sign one owes you statutory damages.
  2. On the due date: if you haven't been paid, send a polite reminder. Our payment reminder emails escalate step by step.
  3. A few weeks late: send a firm reminder citing the law and the double damages it provides, with the late fees your contract allows.
  4. Still unpaid: file a complaint with the agency that enforces the law, or go to small claims court.

General information, not legal advice. Laws, thresholds and agencies change: check the official sources below.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Freelance Isn't Free Act?
A law that protects freelancers from late and missing payments. New York City adopted it in 2017 and New York State extended it statewide on August 28, 2024. Clients must sign a written contract for work worth $800 or more (alone or combined with work in the previous 120 days), pay by the date in the contract or within 30 days of completion, and can't retaliate. Late payment can cost them double damages.
Which states protect freelancers?
New York and Illinois have statewide laws, and California's Freelance Worker Protection Act took effect on January 1, 2025. Cities such as New York City, Los Angeles, Minneapolis, Seattle and Columbus, Ohio have their own ordinances. Elsewhere, your contract and general contract law protect you.
What must the written contract include?
Under the New York, Illinois and California laws: both parties' names and mailing addresses, an itemized list of the services, their value, the rate and method of pay, the payment date or how it will be set, and the date by which you must submit your invoice. Our freelance contract generator covers these points.
What can I do if a covered client pays late?
Send a written reminder that cites the law, then file a complaint with the agency in charge (for example the NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection or the New York State Department of Labor) or sue. Courts can award double damages for late payment, plus attorney's fees.
Does the law apply if I work remotely for an out-of-state client?
It depends on the law and on where the work is performed or the client is based. In general, these laws protect freelancers working in the area or hired by businesses located there. When in doubt, contact the agency that enforces the law.
