What a good NDA covers

  • The purpose: the information may be used for this purpose only, so describe it precisely.
  • What is confidential, and what is not: public information, what you already knew, what a third party gave you freely and what you developed on your own are excluded.
  • How long it lasts: a disclosure period, then a number of years of confidentiality.
  • The end of the discussions: the information is returned or destroyed, and nobody is obliged to sign a contract.

Sign the agreement before sharing anything, mark your documents “Confidential” and keep a record of what you sent and when. When the project goes ahead, write the freelance contract.

Frequently asked questions

When should a freelancer sign an NDA?
Before any confidential information changes hands: when a client wants to share product plans, code, data or customer lists before hiring you, or when you pitch your own idea to a partner. Once the project is signed, the confidentiality clause of your freelance contract may be enough.
Mutual or one-way NDA?
Use a one-way agreement when only one side shares confidential information, and a mutual agreement when both do. The generator sets the roles for you: the Discloser shares the information, the Recipient receives it and must protect it.
Why does the template include a Defend Trade Secrets Act notice?
Under the Defend Trade Secrets Act, an employer must give notice of whistleblower immunity in any agreement with an employee that governs confidential information, and “employee” includes an individual working as a contractor or consultant. Without the notice, the employer cannot recover exemplary damages or attorney fees from that individual under the Act. The notice is always included, whoever shares the information.
How long should confidentiality last?
Choose a length based on how long the information stays valuable. The template protects information shared during the disclosure period for the number of years you pick after that period ends. A trade secret keeps its legal protection only while its owner takes reasonable measures to keep it secret; the USPTO lists confidentiality agreements signed before access, and the return of materials, among those measures.
Is this template legal advice?
No. It covers common business-to-business discussions. For a high-value secret, an invention you plan to patent, an agreement under foreign law or terms the other side wants to negotiate, have a lawyer review it.
