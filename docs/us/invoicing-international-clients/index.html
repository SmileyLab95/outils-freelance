Your client

    What to do for this client

      In the US

        The rule in one sentence

        For a service, foreign VAT generally follows the client's country when the client is a business, and that business accounts for it itself, while sales to private customers can make you liable for the tax of their country, from the first sale for digital services in the EU and the UK, and above a threshold in Canada.

        Good to know

        • Check that your client is a business before leaving the tax off: its VAT or GST/HST number, or documents showing its activity.
        • Your US taxes don't change: income from foreign clients is taxed like any other, and self-employment tax applies to it. The self-employment tax calculator estimates both.
        • Currency, payment terms and bank fees are yours to agree: put them in your contract.

        Information for guidance only, based on IRS and FinCEN guidance, the European Commission, HMRC and the Canada Revenue Agency. For an unusual situation, check with the tax authority concerned or an accountant.

        Frequently asked questions

        Do I charge VAT or sales tax to a client abroad?
        Not to a business client in the EU or the UK: for services, the client accounts for any VAT itself (reverse charge). Private customers are different: EU and UK VAT is due from your first sale of automated digital services, and Canada taxes services and digital products sold to its consumers once your sales there go over C$30,000 in 12 months. US sales tax is set by each state, and most states don't tax most professional services.
        How do I report income paid in euros, pounds or Canadian dollars?
        In US dollars, on Schedule C like any other income. Convert each payment at the exchange rate on the day you receive it, or use the IRS yearly average rate if you're paid evenly through the year. The IRS has no official rate: use a posted rate, and the same method every time.
        My client's country withheld tax on my payment: can I get it back?
        Ask your client to apply the reduced rate of its tax treaty with the US, using Form 6166, a certificate of US residency you request from the IRS with Form 8802. A foreign tax credit (Form 1116) only offsets US tax on foreign-source income: if you did the work in the US, the income is US-source and the credit usually doesn't help, unless the treaty says otherwise.
        Do I have to file an FBAR?
        Yes, if you hold financial accounts outside the US worth more than $10,000 in total at any time in the year. The FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) is filed with FinCEN, not with your tax return, by April 15, with an automatic extension to October 15.
        Will my foreign client send me a Form 1099?
        Don't count on it. Report all your income from foreign clients whether or not you receive a Form 1099.
