How the comparison works

On the employee side, the calculator starts from your gross salary and takes off your share of FICA (Social Security up to the wage base, Medicare, and the Additional Medicare Tax on high wages) and federal income tax after the standard deduction. Your employer pays the other half of FICA and unemployment tax on top of your salary: they aren't part of your income.

On the freelance side, it looks for the revenue that leaves the same income as a sole proprietor, after your expenses, the self-employment tax (both halves of Social Security and Medicare) and federal income tax, with half of the self-employment tax and the qualified business income deduction taken off. Divided by your billed hours, it gives the equivalent hourly rate. The self-employment tax calculator shows the full detail, and the S corp comparison what changes with a company.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to bill to replace my salary?
With a $70,000 salary, a single filer keeps $58,075 after FICA and federal income tax. As a sole proprietor with no expenses, you need $73,141 in revenue to keep the same amount, or $56.79 an hour over 1,288 billed hours.
Why does my freelance rate need to be higher than my hourly wage?
Because you only bill part of your working time: vacation, holidays, finding clients and admin aren't paid. You also pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare through the self-employment tax, and your own benefits. The calculator divides the revenue you need by the hours you actually bill (1,288 by default, the assumption of the hourly rate calculator).
Are employer benefits included?
Only if you enter them: health insurance premiums paid by your employer, a 401(k) match and other benefits have no standard amount. Add their yearly value under “Employer-paid benefits” and the calculator raises the revenue you need to cover them.
What about state income tax?
It isn't included, on either side: rates and rules depend on your state. The comparison covers FICA, the self-employment tax and federal income tax, with the standard deduction and, for the freelancer, the qualified business income deduction.
