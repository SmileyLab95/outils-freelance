Before you switch

By default, the IRS treats a single-member LLC like a sole proprietorship: its income still goes on your Schedule C, so forming one mainly changes your liability protection. Electing S corporation status changes your federal taxes: see whether it's worth it with the sole proprietor vs S corp calculator, which counts payroll and filing costs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I change from sole proprietor to LLC?
File articles of organization with your state, with a registered agent in the state, then check your business licenses, open a bank account in the LLC's name and keep its funds separate. Registering usually costs less than $300, depending on the state.
Does my LLC need a new EIN?
A single-member LLC can keep using your sole proprietor EIN if it doesn't elect to be taxed as a corporation or S corporation, has no employees and owes no excise tax. Otherwise, it needs its own EIN.
When is Form 2553 due?
No more than 2 months and 15 days after the beginning of the tax year the S corporation election should apply to, or at any time during the year before. An LLC that files Form 2553 doesn't also need Form 8832.
Do I have to pay myself a salary in an S corp?
Yes: an S corporation must pay a shareholder who works in the business reasonable compensation before making other distributions, through payroll. There's no fixed formula.
