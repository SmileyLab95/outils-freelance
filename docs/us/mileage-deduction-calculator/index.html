Standard mileage rate or actual expenses?

With the standard rate you multiply your business miles by the IRS rate, which covers gas, maintenance, insurance and depreciation. With the actual expense method you deduct the business share of all your car costs. The standard rate is simpler and often better for fuel-efficient cars; actual expenses can be higher for expensive vehicles driven mostly for business.

Frequently asked questions

What is the IRS mileage rate for 2026?
72.5 cents per business mile from January 1, 2026 (IR-2025-128). The IRS later announced a higher rate of 76 cents per mile for business miles driven from July 1, 2026; check irs.gov for the latest figure before you file.
Who can use the standard mileage rate?
Self-employed people and sole proprietors who use their own car for business. You must choose the standard rate in the first year you use the car for business; after that you can switch between the standard rate and actual expenses in most cases.
What counts as business miles?
Driving between client sites, to meet clients, to pick up supplies or between your home office and other work locations. Commuting from home to a regular workplace doesn't count unless your home is your main place of business.
What records do I need?
A mileage log with the date, destination, business purpose and miles of each trip, made at or near the time of the trip, plus receipts for parking and tolls.
