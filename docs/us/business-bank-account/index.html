Do you need a business bank account?

Sole proprietor, LLC or corporation: why a separate account matters, whether you need an EIN to open it and how long to keep your records.

      Good habits with a business account

      • All business money in, all business expenses out: deposit every business receipt and pay business expenses from it.
      • Paying yourself: the IRS suggests writing checks to yourself only when you take money out of the business for personal use.
      • Reconcile every month with your records: it makes your quarterly estimated taxes much easier.

      Frequently asked questions

      Do I need a business bank account as a sole proprietor?
      It's not a legal requirement, but the IRS says opening a business checking account is one of the first things to do when you start: keep it separate from your personal account and deposit all your business receipts in it.
      Do I need an EIN to open a business bank account?
      Not as a sole proprietor: you can use your Social Security number. A single-member LLC with no employees doesn't need an EIN, but can get one to open the account. A partnership or a corporation, including an LLC with two or more members, must have one. An EIN is free.
      Why does a separate account matter for an LLC?
      Keeping business funds separate from personal funds is part of what protects your personal assets when you have an LLC or a corporation, according to the SBA.
      How long should I keep bank statements?
      Generally 3 years from the date you file your return, longer in some cases, such as 6 years if you left out income over 25% of the gross income you reported.
